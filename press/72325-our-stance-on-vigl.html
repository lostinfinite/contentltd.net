Our Stance on Vigilante Groups

ContentLTD  |  July 2025
ContentLTD has a clear responsibility to protect its users, especially minors and vulnerable individuals. We take that responsibility seriously. Our moderation team reviews all reports submitted through https://contentltd.net/support, and we take enforcement action where there are violations of our Terms of Service, Community Standards, or applicable laws.

We do not permit any content that depicts, promotes, or facilitates real-world child sexual abuse, rape, or other forms of criminal exploitation. Any user found posting or distributing such material is banned and, where appropriate, reported to law enforcement.

What we will not do is allow third parties with no legal authority to hijack our moderation process, threaten our infrastructure, or intimidate our providers. This includes activist and advocacy organizations that use emotional pressure, public shaming, or coordinated campaigns to force private companies into deplatforming content or users that do not violate any laws or platform policies.

One recent example is the July 2025 open letter published by Collective Shout, addressed to executives at Visa, PayPal, Mastercard, Discover, and other financial institutions. That letter accuses payment processors of “facilitating and profiting from rape, incest and child sexual abuse game sales,” based largely on the existence of disturbing or adult-themed fictional games found on open marketplaces like Steam and Itch.io. Their request is blunt: stop allowing payments for entire platforms based on the presence of content they personally find offensive or unacceptable, even if it is legal and removed after proper review.

We reject this approach.

Collective Shout is not a regulator, law enforcement agency, or judicial body. They do not get to define what is or isn’t lawful or policy-violating on platforms they do not operate. While we are fully aligned in wanting to protect children from abuse, we will not conflate advocacy with authority. Their open letter is not evidence, not a legal finding, and not a substitute for policy-driven enforcement.

This kind of campaign is exactly what we refer to as vigilante interference—where third parties attempt to bypass law and platform policy by pressuring intermediaries like payment processors, hosts, or registrars to act out of fear, not principle.

ContentLTD will never remove content or ban users based solely on ideological complaints or activist pressure. Unless we receive:

  • A valid legal takedown notice
  • A violation report that clearly breaches our ToS or Community Standards
  • Or a lawful order from a competent court or law enforcement body
we will not take moderation action.

We also won’t tolerate threats made toward our partners. If a third party pressures our payment processor, we will consider that an attempt at business interference and will treat it accordingly.

ContentLTD stands by a simple principle: rules apply equally and are enforced consistently. External organizations—no matter how loud—don’t get to rewrite those rules because they disagree with fictional content, artistic expression, or a platform’s existence. If something breaks the law, the correct response is to report it to the authorities, not to harass infrastructure providers into cutting off lawful platforms.

We protect real people, not reputations built on outrage.

To report a valid concern, visit https://contentltd.net/support. If you're a law enforcement agency, please follow the process outlined on our contact page. Everyone else is expected to respect the difference between enforcement and interference.
